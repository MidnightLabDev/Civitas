# Resources, economic exchange, ecology and technological change

The goal is coherent accounting and credible feedback, not attaching an economy label to every invented event.

## Material stocks and budgets

Use separate ledgers for food, water, energy, raw inputs, land, capital, goods, compute, technical capacity, and waste where relevant. For every resource, state its unit, replenishment, extraction, conversion, storage loss, exchange, and degradation. Include resource use by infrastructure and maintenance. Do not permit hidden infinite supplies.

## Institutions of exchange

Compare barter, markets, credit, taxation, public provisioning, reciprocal exchange, cooperatives, algorithmic allocation and nonmarket commons. Specify ownership or access rights, contract enforcement, prices, bargaining power, monopoly, public goods, and risk sharing. Trade gains are conditional, distribution matters, and trade corridors create vulnerabilities.

## Technology and knowledge

Research investment competes with consumption, maintenance and safety budgets. Some knowledge is reproducible, some tacit, some embodied in institutions, and some stored in fragile infrastructure. Adoption is not invention; training and incentives matter. Model diffusion with social networks and supply constraints, not just a global technology level counter.

## Ecology and long horizons

Ask what regenerative limits, waste disposal, biodiversity, climate exposure and water constraints the system faces. Distinguish renewable resources, exhaustible stock, and renewable resources that can be degraded by overuse. Model delayed damage and restoration cost.

## Macroeconomic patterns to test

Specialization, economies of scale, inequality, concentration, innovation incentives, path dependence, demographic pressure, debt fragility, unemployment, economic buffers and dependency networks. These are hypotheses in a synthetic civilization, not universal predictions for a real economy.

## Example original accounting model

For energy `E[t+1] = max(0, E[t] + produced - household_use - industry_use - maintenance_use - losses)`. Production depends on capital, resource inputs and infrastructure condition. Report shortages as unmet demand rather than hiding them by clipping outputs. When analyzing policy, compare who receives the scarce units.

## Resilience design

Build redundancy and diversified supply, keep emergency reserves, encourage institutional learning, allow peaceful trade and migration, provide failure containment, audit shared stocks, and test dependence on dominant suppliers.

## What to measure

Survival, accessible services, production, unmet demand, volatility, ecological depletion, distribution, institutional trust, maintenance backlog, investment capacity and recovery time after shocks. No single prosperity score replaces all these measurements.

## Additional models and cautions (1.0)

- **Lock-in** (Arthur 1989): increasing returns can lock in inferior technologies; early interventions matter more than late ones.
- **Population-idea feedback** (Kremer 1993) for long-run growth; breaks after demographic transition.
- **Overshoot and collapse** (Meadows et al. 1972; empirical comparison Herrington 2021, contested): delays plus degradable capacity produce boom-bust.
- **Lucas critique**: relationships estimated under one policy regime shift when policy changes; behavioural models must include adaptation.
- **Commons with regrowth**: maximum sustainable yield `rK/4`; `aisociety` uses it as an entrenched constitutional floor.
- **Wealth concentration without skill differences**: multiplicative exchange concentrates wealth; redistribution stabilizes (QUANT_MODELS).
- **Shocks**: correlated shocks (drought plus price spike plus conflict) matter more than independent ones; model correlation explicitly.
