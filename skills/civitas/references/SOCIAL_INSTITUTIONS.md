# Society, institutions, norms, power and collective choice

A civilization is not solely a technology tree. Map relations among institutions, incentives, culture, power, environment and individual choices. Social theory offers **contending models**, not universally valid laws.

## Institutions and legitimacy

Define formal rules, informal norms, enforcement, decision authority, succession, transparency, entry and exit, public goods, conflict resolution and checks on powerful groups. Distinguish legal validity, social acceptance, procedural fairness and practical enforcement. For an institution, ask who writes the rules, who is excluded, who audits, and what happens when the rules lose support.

## Cooperation and the commons

Shared resources can suffer exploitation, but mutual governance can succeed. Elinor Ostrom's research challenges a simplistic assumption that every shared resource inevitably collapses. Examine membership, clearly defined boundaries where appropriate, participatory rule formation, monitoring, graduated remedies, dispute resolution and nested governance; these are design prompts, not a universal checklist or legal prescription.

## Incentives and strategic behavior

Use repeated games, coordination games, public goods problems, principal agent problems, free riding, information asymmetry, credible commitment and mechanism design. For each rule, ask:

- Which behaviors are rewarded even if nobody intended them?
- Do actors have the means and information to comply?
- Can powerful actors rewrite the rules?
- What happens when rules scale beyond the founding community?
- Can credible dissent, whistleblowing or peaceful exit continue?

## Culture and demography

Represent demographic transitions, language, education, family and migration as evolving, contextual factors. Avoid fixed stereotypes or treating demographic identity as destiny. For fictional societies, build cultures around history, geography, institutions and material conditions, with internal disagreement and exceptions.

## Information and belief

Trust depends on source quality, institutional memory and communication networks. Model rumor diffusion, censorship incentives, network clustering, polarization and scientific consensus only with clear mechanisms. A rumor propagation simulation does not validate claims about any real group. Do not build manipulation plans aimed at political or vulnerable audiences.

## Social mobility and inequality

Distinguish distribution of income, assets, opportunity, voice, information, technology access, and ability to leave a system. Consider whether efficiency increases aggregate output while worsening exclusion. Measure power concentration, capture risk, procedural justice and externalities, not only average welfare.

## Institutional lifecycle

Founding coalition → enforcement and norm creation → expansion → specialization → hierarchy or polycentric organization → stress → adaptation, fragmentation, reform or dissolution. This is an optional organizing pattern, not a universal sequence.

## Tests of an institution

Role reversal, minority rights, separation of powers, loss of key founders, information blackout, resource shortage, technological breakthrough, multi generation succession, compliance burden, member exit, audit capture and emergency powers sunset.

## Additional mechanisms (1.0)

- **Preference falsification** (Kuran 1991): stable-looking regimes can fall suddenly when hidden opposition becomes visible; model with threshold cascades (`diffusion`).
- **Costly punishment and institutions**: peer punishment sustains cooperation in experiments (Fehr & Gächter 2000); central sanctioning depends on legitimacy and integrity. `society` shows legitimacy collapse under high corruption [E3].
- **Inclusive vs extractive institutions** (Acemoglu & Robinson): a lens with debated causal claims.
- **Elite dynamics**: elite overproduction and intra-elite competition (SDT) as drivers of instability.
- **Self-fulfilling and self-defeating prophecies** (Merton 1948): public forecasts change behaviour.
- **In-group bias and polarization**: imitation that discounts out-groups produces divergent group norms (`society` `ingroup_bias`, `group_cooperation_gap`).
- **Mechanism design**: design rules so that truthful, cooperative behaviour is each actor's best response; verify with simulated adversaries.
